GoFundGeo updates

Terra Draw, £1000

The GoFundGeo grant has been pivotal in moving the Terra Draw project forward. Two concrete examples this year are the introduction of the widely requested undo/redo functionality, which has been very well received by the community, and support for dashed lines, another feature that users have been requesting for some time. I’m (James Milner) very grateful to OSGeo:UK for their continued support of the project. Their backing has played an important role in helping Terra Draw grow, evolve, and continue delivering useful new functionality to the wider open-source geospatial community.


gitRmap, £500

Receiving £500 from GoFundGeo for gitRmap has been really helpful as it has allowed me (Nick Bearman) to dedicate time to developing the gitRmap code into a usable tool - have a look at nickbearman.com/gitRmap for more details and info on how to use it! I shared the tool at the Open Science Retreat earlier this year, where it was well received and was used by a couple of the groups to create a useful map. It has also enabled me to present gitRmap at two upcoming conferences (inc. FOSS4G 2026 in Hiroshima, Japan) to get some more feedback on the tool and hopefully get some more users!


GeoNetwork, £1000

GeoNetwork 5 is a major upgrade to the widely used open-source geospatial catalogue platform. The new version will address key security and sustainability challenges by moving to Java 17 and associated technologies, while also introducing a modern XML engine and support for OGC API – Records. Development will be delivered through a series of funded milestones, providing working functionality at each stage before ultimately delivering a standalone replacement for GeoNetwork 4. OSGeo:UK has contributed funding to help ensure the GeoNetwork remains a secure, modern and sustainable open-source platform for the future.


qgis2web, £1000

In December 2025 Andrea released V3.31.0 of qgis2web. By August 2026 the project was on release V4.3.0. The project goes from strength to strength and has fixed issues on (amongst other things) mobile, in OpenLayers and Leaflet, label control, and has made itself QGIS 4 ready. A big update in the latest release is the implementation of PBF Vector Tile export which enables smoother support for large projects and improved responsiveness all round: this is probably the biggest architectural change since qgis2web was created: the processing time moves from map loading to map generation, producing web maps that remain fast regardless of the size of the original data.


pgRouting & QGIS

pgRouting and QGIS, both critical projects, continue to be developed thanks to sustaining members such as OSGeo:UK.


GoFundGeo GISRUK Award 2026, £200

A Leeds Institute for Data Analytics (LIDA) data scientist, Chenrui Xiao, won the GoFundGeo GISRUK grant to develop an open-source approach to measuring “wheelability”, or the accessibility of urban environments for wheelchair users and people using other wheeled mobility aids. The project uses high-resolution 3D mobile-mapping data to identify features such as dropped kerbs, gradients and pavement characteristics, incorporating them into a spatial network to assess accessibility across Bradford. Building on collaboration with Healthy Urban Places and the University of Bradford’s Visualising Heritage team, the work aims to turn this research into a reusable geospatial tool that could help cities better understand barriers to inclusive mobility and improve street design.

Wider Geospatial News

FOSS4G Hiroshima 2026 takes place from 30 August to 5 September, bringing the global open geospatial community together in Japan under the theme “Bridging Geospatial Technology and Humanity”. The programme includes two days of hands-on workshops, three days of conference sessions, a community sprint, networking and community events, with a particular focus on how open-source geospatial technologies can help address humanitarian, environmental and societal challenges.


Then the baton is passed to Bristol, with OSGeo:UK hosting FOSS4G 2027 from 23–29 August. The event will be based at the University of Bristol, with workshops on Monday and Tuesday followed by three days of conference sessions, giving the UK community a fantastic opportunity to put open geospatial technology centre stage and welcome the global FOSS4G community to the South West.


Other OSGeo news

A recent Birds of a Feather session at FOSS4G Europe in Timișoara explored the growing use of AI in OSGeo projects, with developers sharing both positive experiences and concerns about AI-generated contributions. The discussion highlighted the need for respectful, human-centred collaboration alongside AI-assisted development, and called for shared guidance, skills and tools to help projects make effective use of AI. If you have an opinion on this then come along to FOSS4G:UK in October and discuss it in the Unconference session, or in the various networking opportunities.


OSGeo has established a new European legal entity, Stichting Open Source Geospatial, based in Wageningen in the Netherlands, alongside its existing US foundation. The move gives OSGeo access to European research and innovation funding, potentially makes donations easier for European supporters, and strengthens its ability to engage with European institutions while retaining a single, community-led governance structure.
